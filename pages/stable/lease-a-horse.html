Full lease information

How Lease a Horse Works

Please read these details before applying. Every arrangement remains subject to horse suitability, rider assessment, availability and Gallop's welfare approval.

01

5 horses open every month

Gallop will release 5 horses for lease applications each month. This programme is for horses only and does not include ponies. Our objective is to place 30 suitable horses into responsible lease partnerships.

02

Former racehorses with a new purpose

The featured horses may include ex-racehorses that are no longer suitable for competitive racing. They are not presented as broken or without value. They are experienced horses moving into a more balanced life of care, suitable riding and dependable human connection.

03

S$2,000 monthly lease and care contribution

The lease fee is S$2,000 per month for one horse. It helps support housing, staffing, routine care and feeding. A lessee does not purchase or take ownership of the horse; the horse remains under Gallop's management.

04

S$2,500 monthly corporate lease

Companies and organisations may take part through a corporate lease at S$2,500 per month. The arrangement supports the horse's welfare and care but does not allow the horse to be used as a commercial prop, paid attraction or business asset without Gallop's written approval.

05

Training before independent access

Before leasing begins, the rider receives training to confirm safe handling and riding. The full package provides 56 hours of horse-care training for the registered lessee. Training beyond the included allocation must be paid for separately.

06

Lessons and family privileges

The registered lessee receives 5 free riding lessons. Immediate family members receive 20% off eligible riding lessons, riding activities and selected merchandise. Benefits cannot be resold, transferred or used by unrelated guests.

07

Only one horse for each lessee

One person may lease only one horse at a time. This protects consistency and makes the relationship about learning the needs, behaviour and care routine of one individual horse.

Horse welfare comes first

What You Can Do When You Lease a Horse

You may ride, groom, care for and spend time with your leased horse within the approved schedule. Access is generous, but it is never unlimited when the horse needs rest or the stable is running lessons.

Maximum riding time

Your horse must not be ridden for more than 2 hours in one day. A preferred balanced routine is approximately 45 minutes in the morning and another 45 minutes in the afternoon, with proper rest between sessions.

Free-and-easy riding

Lessees may enjoy free-and-easy riding during suitable quieter periods, especially in the morning. The arena, weather, horse condition and staff instructions determine whether riding can proceed.

Respect peak lesson times

During busy lesson periods, Gallop's scheduled classes have priority. Lessees must not enter, interrupt or disturb a lesson and may be asked to wait or use another approved time.

Protect rest time

No visits from 12:00 PM to 2:00 PM or from 7:00 PM onwards. These are protected rest periods for the horses.

Arrive properly dressed

Anyone visiting or handling the horse must wear proper stable attire. Riders must follow Gallop's requirements for helmets, covered footwear, riding clothing and other safety equipment.

Care, do not commercialise

The horse remains under Gallop's care. Lessees may not sublease the horse or use the relationship for lessons, paid rides, breeding, content hire or any other business.

No unauthorised users

The horse is matched to the registered lessee. Friends, relatives or customers may not ride the horse unless Gallop has specifically assessed and approved the activity.

If your horse is injured

If Your Leased Horse Is Injured

Gallop will take responsibility for treatment costs covered under the lease programme. The horse cannot be ridden while injured and the lessee must follow the veterinarian's and Gallop team's instructions.

There will be an initial period of up to 7 days when the lessee may be unable to use the horse. If the horse does not recover sufficiently, is expected to need a longer rehabilitation period, or cannot safely return to the agreed activity, Gallop will review the case and arrange a suitable change of horse where available.

No replacement is made merely for convenience. The decision is based on the injured horse's welfare, veterinary advice and the suitability of another horse for the lessee.
